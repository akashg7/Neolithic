# Kisan Credit Card (KCC) and interest subvention

## What the KCC is
The Kisan Credit Card gives farmers short-term credit for crop needs through banks. Commercial banks, regional rural banks, small finance banks and cooperative banks implement it. Owner cultivators, tenant farmers, oral lessees and sharecroppers can all be covered. The collateral-free limit is Rs 2 lakh per borrower, raised from Rs 1.6 lakh with effect from 1 January 2025.

## Interest rate and the prompt repayment benefit
Short-term crop loans up to Rs 3 lakh carry an interest rate of 7 percent a year. Farmers who repay on time get an extra 3 percent benefit, so the effective rate for a prompt repayer is 4 percent a year. For the 2025-26 year the Reserve Bank of India notified an interest subvention of 1.5 percent paid to lending institutions, plus a prompt repayment incentive of up to 3 percent for farmers. The subvention is claimed by the bank, not by the farmer.

## Which loan limit qualifies for the subvention: sources differ
The RBI circular of 13 January 2026 says the interest subvention and prompt repayment incentive apply on an overall limit of Rs 3 lakh per year, with crop loans taking priority and a Rs 2 lakh sub-limit for farmers who borrow only for allied activities such as dairy or fisheries. A government explainer dated 11 March 2026 says the crop-loan limit under the Modified Interest Subvention Scheme was raised from Rs 3 lakh to Rs 5 lakh. These two sources do not agree, so a farmer should ask the bank which limit applies to their account before borrowing.

## Loans against stored produce
A farmer who holds a KCC can also take a pledge loan against an electronic negotiable warehouse receipt (e-NWR). See the WDRA pledge loan document for the terms.
