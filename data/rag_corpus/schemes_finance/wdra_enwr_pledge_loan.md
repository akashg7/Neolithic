# Pledge loan against electronic warehouse receipt (e-NWR)

## How it works
A farmer deposits produce in a warehouse registered with the Warehousing Development and Regulatory Authority (WDRA). The produce is assayed, and it must meet the WDRA grade for that commodity before an electronic negotiable warehouse receipt (e-NWR) is issued. The receipt is held in an electronic repository (NERL or CCRL). The farmer then pledges the e-NWR to a bank to take a loan, and can sell later, which helps avoid distress selling at harvest. An e-NWR is valid only until the commodity's shelf life ends or the goods are withdrawn.

## Loan terms
The margin the bank keeps is usually 25 percent of the produce value, though it differs from bank to bank. Farmers who hold a Kisan Credit Card can get a pledge loan against an e-NWR at 7 percent a year for six months. The government extended interest subvention to small and marginal farmers with a KCC account for loans against e-NWR.

## Credit guarantee scheme
The Credit Guarantee Scheme for e-NWR based Pledge Financing (CGS-NPF) was launched in December 2024 with a corpus of Rs 1,000 crore. It guarantees banks against default on pledge loans, so farmers, farmer producer organisations, traders and small businesses can borrow against e-NWR without giving additional collateral.

## e-Kisan Upaj Nidhi portal
e-Kisan Upaj Nidhi is an online platform where farmers can apply for post-harvest loans from banks against their e-NWR. WDRA, the food department, the financial services department and NABARD run it together. Banks on the platform show their interest rate and loan amount, so the farmer can compare offers.

## Perishable produce
Some banks do not finance perishable fruit and vegetables under their warehouse receipt loan products, so check with the bank before storing onion for a pledge loan. Fresh onion also has a short e-NWR validity, see the validity-by-crop document.
