# Maharashtra APMC market fee and commission

## Who runs the market
Agricultural produce markets in Maharashtra are regulated by the Maharashtra Agricultural Produce Marketing (Development and Regulation) Act, 1963. A market committee manages each regulated market, licenses commission agents, weighmen and other traders, and levies fees. Lasalgaon in Nashik is the largest wholesale onion market in Asia.

## Market fee
Under section 31 of the Act the market committee can levy a market fee. The fee is paid by the buyer, not the farmer, and must be paid immediately after the produce is weighed. A news report from June 2026 puts the standard market fee at 0.75 percent of the transaction value, which is Rs 75 on every Rs 10,000. That figure is from a news report and not the official notification, so confirm the current rate with your market committee.

## Commission (adat)
The market committee can fix the commission that a commission agent charges, with prior approval of the State Government, and the Act provides for it to be charged to the buyer. In 2016 the State directed that commission should not be charged to farmers, which led to a trader protest in Nashik. Farmers should check their sale receipt and ask the market committee if any commission or fee is deducted from their payment.

## Fees can be waived in special cases
In June 2026 Maharashtra waived the APMC fee for two central agencies buying onion for price stabilisation. This does not change what ordinary buyers pay.
