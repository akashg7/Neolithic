# WDRA fresh onion quality standards

## Grades
WDRA grades fresh onion as Extra class, Class I and Class II. Extra class allows 5 percent of the lot (by number or weight) to miss the grade but still meet Class I. Class I allows 10 percent that miss the grade but meet Class II. Class II allows 10 percent that meet only the minimum requirements. In Class II, early shoot growth visible from outside is limited to 10 percent by number or weight.

## Size
Size is the maximum equatorial diameter of the bulb. The size codes are A (10 to 20 mm), B (21 to 40 mm), C (41 to 70 mm) and D (71 mm and above). The minimum diameter is 10 mm. Across all grades, 10 percent by weight may fall outside the declared size, but no more than 20 percent below or above it.

## Stems
Stems must be twisted or cleanly cut and no longer than 6 cm, except stringed onions. A string must hold at least six onions with fully dried stems.

## Range designations for defects
For industrial or processing use, WDRA also defines Range-1, Range-2 and Range-3 by the maximum share of defects by count.
- Cut bulbs (5 to 15 mm long): 5 percent, 10 percent, 15 percent.
- Double split bulbs: 2 percent, 5 percent, and above 8 percent.
- Sprouted bulbs: 2 percent, 5 percent, and above 8 percent.
- Rooting: 2 percent, 5 percent, and above 8 percent.
- Maximum bulb diameter: up to 65 mm, up to 45 mm, and up to 25 mm.

## What the standard does not give
The WDRA onion standard has no numeric limit for moisture or foreign matter. It only requires onions to be clean, free of visible foreign matter and frost damage, and practically free of pests. A moisture limit used in the Krishi-Mitr platform grading is a prototype rule, not a WDRA number.

## Shelf life
Fresh onion shelf life is 2 months, with an initial e-NWR validity of 1 month.
